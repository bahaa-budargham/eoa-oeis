# eoa-oeis: The Alphabet as a Sequence Engine

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23858517.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23858517)
[![License: CC BY 4.0](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-CC_BY_4.0-lightgrey.svg)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
[![ORCID](https://img.shields.io/badge/ORCID-0009--0005--2955--3703-green.svg)](https://orcid.org/0009-0005-2955-3703)

Auditing the integer sequences generated across 36 human writing systems under the **Engineering of Alphabets (EOA)** deterministic recursive operator, evaluated term-by-term against the **On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (OEIS)**.

Every sequence is computed to 500 terms in exact BigInt arithmetic from open incidence matrices.

---

## Headline Census Numbers

* **36 Writing Systems** evaluated across **1,056 individual letters**.
* **402 Distinct Integer Sequences** produced across all scripts.
* **56 OEIS-Matched Sequences** (covering 498 letters).
* **346 Uncatalogued Sequences** (covering 558 letters), each satisfying an explicit integer linear recurrence derived from its matrix.
* **Zero Cherry-Picking:** All 36 scripts are retained in the census, including 8 scripts that matched nothing in the OEIS (large tangled graphs or flat collapses).

## Statistical Controls & Structural Anomalies

1. **The Small-Core Anomaly ($0 / 250$):**  
   Natural scripts isolate growth into tiny recurrent hubs (2 to 4 letters). Across 250 matched random-alphabet simulations (2–5 letter names), not a single random alphabet formed a small core (core sizes ran from 21 to 26).
2. **Multi-Lineage Universality ($p = 0.009$):**  
   The growth factor $\phi^2$ recurs across three historically independent writing traditions (Semitic, Brahmic, and Japonic). Against an exhaustive universe of 4,482 non-negative $3 \times 3$ matrices, this multi-lineage recurrence is statistically significant at $p = 0.009$ (1 in 110).
3. **The English E5 Spectral Split ($0 / 100$):**  
   The 26×26 English E5 matrix factors into 13 linear factors and one irreducible polynomial of degree 13: $\text{charpoly}(M) = (x - 2) \cdot x^3 \cdot (x - 1)^9 \cdot G_2(x)$. Zero of 100 matched random matrices produced an equal split.

## Repository Files

* `census.csv` — Full census of all 402 distinct sequences generated by the 36 scripts.
* `uncatalogued_sequences.csv` — The 346 sequences currently absent from the OEIS, with exact recurrences and initial terms.
* `census_summary.json` — Machine-readable summary statistics.
* `verify_wows.py` — Standalone script reproducing term-exact OEIS identity matches.
* `audit.py` — Reproduces the 402-sequence distinct count from the raw matrices.
* `e5_split_test_sage.sage` — SageMath script certifying the E5 characteristic polynomial factorization.
